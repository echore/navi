<div class="lang-en">
<p>By chance, I started using Claude Code and found it genuinely useful. So I recommended it to people around me. Most of them weren&#39;t interested at all.  <br>A friend&#39;s response stopped me: &quot;Why would I bother? Life and work are already exhausting. Now I have to learn how to use another tool? Not just that, I also need to pay 30 bucks? &quot;<br>That hit me completely.</p>
<p>I remembered exactly why I hadn&#39;t wanted to try any of these tools myself. I avoided Claude code and Codex for months because both names contain the word &quot;Code.&quot;</p>
<p>I&#39;m not a developer. I assumed they weren&#39;t for me. That was completely wrong. But I don&#39;t think it was stupid. The tools never told us otherwise.</p>
<p>This is the clearest example why ‘advanced’ AI adoption is stalling, and it has nothing to do with people being unwilling to learn.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>There are two real barriers.</p>
<p>The first is that the tools aren&#39;t genuinely easy yet. &quot;Easy&quot; doesn&#39;t mean simple UI. It means you open it, the value is obvious, and you don&#39;t need to spend a weekend configuring a workflow before it helps you. They haven&#39;t crossed that line. You have to meet them halfway, and a lot of people reasonably decide not to.</p>
<p>The second barrier is noise. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, new features every day, new products every week. The people building these move fast because they have to. But for everyone else, it just feels like homework. </p>
<p>So people wait. Which, honestly, might be the right call. The tools are converging. In a year, half of this will be seamlessly integrated.</p>
<p>Using AI as chatbot  became the default not because it was technically superior, but because &quot;just talk to it&quot; is a model anyone can do it.</p>
<p>If you&#39;ve been ignoring a tool because of how it&#39;s named or positioned, it might be worth a second look. The gap between what these products are called and what they can actually do is wider than most people realize.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>I know this is a pain point for people without a technical background, which is part of why I started putting this content out. Most tutorials today focus on setup, technical configuration rather than solving a specific, concrete problem, and most are aimed at developers. My core focus is AI for non-technical users: people who are complete beginners, no setup talk, just using tools to solve real problems. </p>
<p>I also believe the shift from AI tools built for developers and enterprise users toward general audiences is already underway. I want to document that transition as it happens, and grow through it alongside everyone else.</p>

<div class="lang-zh">
<p>说实话，我之前一直没有在第一时间用 Claude 和 Codex 这样的产品</p>
<p>谜面就在谜底上。因为这两个东西都有一个词叫做“Code”，我就一直以为它只适用于编程（Coding）。而我的一些工作并不是开发，我本人也不是开发背景，并不是一个经常使用 Coding 的人。</p>
<p>所以我觉得：这个工具跟我没有任何关系，我为什么要学呢？既然跟我没关系，我就不学。</p>
<p>后来我才发现，完全不是这样的。它可以做很多很多事情，它很值得去用，无论是谁。</p>
<p>但是现实中。ChatGPT、Claude、Gemini，每隔几天就有新功能、新产品出来。你还没弄懂上一个，下一个又来了。对大多数人来说，这很容易焦虑，那就不用了。</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>所以我觉得，现在很多人没有真正用上这样类似的AI工具，背后其实是两个原因：</p>
<p><strong>第一，产品还没有做到真正的「开箱即用」。</strong><br>好用应该是打开就懂，不用思考就能用，不是看完教程才懂。现在大多数工具还做不到这一点，你得花时间摸索，花时间配置，才能感受到它真正的价值。很多人卡在这一步就放弃了，这不是她们的问题。当然产品本身的核心用户定位也是专业开发者，这不冲突。</p>
<p><strong>第二，没有人帮你过滤。</strong><br>不是缺内容，而是缺「这个东西到底适不适合我」的答案。铺天盖地的测评和推荐，说的都是功能，很少有人告诉你：如果你是做XX的，你真正需要的是这一个解法。就像cat wu在播客里讲的，功能太多了，对于新用户来说想要解决一个问题，很难快速找到用哪一个功能。</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>如果你也因为一个工具的名字、或者「感觉太难了」而放弃过某个 AI 产品，可以回头看看。我也是在朋友强烈安利下开始使用然后就一发不可收拾。我也知道这对于非技术背景的人来说这是一个痛点，所以这也是开启这个账号输出的初衷。</p>
<p>这个账号我会核心围绕 AI For Non-tech users 也就是非技术纯小白使用AI工具，不谈技术设置，专注利用工具解决现实问题。我也相信从AI 工具专门针对专业开发，企业用户一定会拓展到普罗大众，而这个转变正在发生，我希望可以记录这一切的发生，共同进步。</p>
